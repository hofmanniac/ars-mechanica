## Overview

This combination adds an action economy layer to area control, where every territorial action costs action points. Players must budget limited AP across competing strategic priorities: expansion, reinforcement, and contesting opponents.

## How It Works

Action points drive all territorial decisions:

**Common AP costs**:
- **Move unit**: 1 AP per unit or per space moved
- **Place new unit**: 1-2 AP depending on unit type/location
- **Claim territory**: 1-3 AP based on territory value
- **Reinforce position**: 1 AP per additional unit
- **Combat**: 1-2 AP to attack, variable for special abilities

**Typical turn structure**:
1. Receive 4-6 action points
2. Spend AP on territorial actions
3. Resolve conflicts and score territories
4. Refresh for next turn

**Strategic options**:
- **Aggressive expansion**: Spend AP claiming new territories
- **Consolidation**: Reinforce existing holdings
- **Surgical strikes**: Focus AP on contesting key areas
- **Mobility**: Move units to threaten multiple areas

## Why It Works

The AP system creates compelling tactical choices:

- **Resource allocation**: Every AP spent here can't be spent there
- **Efficiency rewards**: Identifying high-value moves maximizes AP impact
- **Breadth vs. depth**: Spread AP wide or concentrate on key areas?
- **Action sequencing**: Move order often matters in area control
- **Tempo control**: AP budgets create consistent turn pacing

Players can't do everything they want, forcing meaningful prioritization of strategic goals.

## Design Considerations

**AP budget sizing**: Too few AP (2-3) limits meaningful multi-area strategies. Too many (10+) reduces opportunity cost. Aim for 4-7 AP allowing 2-4 meaningful actions.

**Cost differentiation**: Vary AP costs to create interesting decisions. If all actions cost 1 AP, it's just "take N actions." Mix 1 AP, 2 AP, and occasional 3+ AP actions.

**Territory values**: High-value territories should be contested. If AP costs to contest don't scale with value, dominant positions become unassailable.

**Movement economy**: Track whether movement costs per unit or per space. Per-unit is simpler but limits tactical repositioning. Per-space rewards planning efficient paths.

**Leftover AP**: What happens to unspent AP? Allow flexible "1 AP for anything" actions or small bonuses to prevent waste.

**Board size**: Larger boards require more AP for movement or movement becomes prohibitively expensive. Scale AP budgets to board size.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in area control games where players have limited actions per turn and must decide how to allocate those actions across territorial objectives, movement, and unit placement.
