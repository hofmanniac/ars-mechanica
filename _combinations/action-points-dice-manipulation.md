## Overview

This combination introduces controlled randomness into action economy by using dice rolls to determine how many action points players have available, with manipulation mechanics providing strategic control over luck.

## How It Works

Dice determine your action budget through various mechanisms:

**Direct conversion**: Roll dice and sum the results for your AP total
- Roll 2d6 = 2-12 action points available this turn
- Roll 3d6, choose 2 highest = 2-12 AP with some control

**Threshold system**: Dice results unlock action tiers
- Roll 1-2: Get 2 AP
- Roll 3-4: Get 3 AP  
- Roll 5-6: Get 4 AP

**Accumulation**: Build AP over multiple rolls
- Roll dice each phase, total determines available actions
- Carry forward unused AP or start fresh each round

**Manipulation options**:
- Rerolls (spend resources to roll again)
- Die modification (add/subtract from results)
- Die locking (keep some dice, reroll others)
- Extra dice (roll more dice, use best results)

## Why It Works

The combination balances luck and strategy effectively:

- **Variable pacing**: High rolls create explosive turns; low rolls require efficiency
- **Risk management**: Manipulation mechanics let skilled players mitigate bad luck
- **Catch-up potential**: Lucky rolls help trailing players compete
- **Excitement factor**: Dice rolling adds drama and memorable moments

Players feel the tension of random outcomes while having enough control to execute strategies over time.

## Design Considerations

**Variance management**: Calculate the AP range from your dice mechanism. Too wide (2-20 AP) creates frustrating swings; too narrow (4-6 AP) feels deterministic. Aim for ±30% variance around the mean.

**Manipulation economy**: Make manipulation cost meaningful but achievable. If manipulation is too cheap, dice become deterministic. Too expensive, and luck dominates.

**Floor and ceiling**: Set minimum AP (even worst roll gives 2-3 AP) so players can always do something meaningful. Cap maximum AP to prevent runaway turns.

**Speed vs. strategy**: Dice rolling can slow turns. Consider simultaneous rolling, or roll at start of previous turn for planning time.

**Turn order**: Does manipulation happen before or after seeing the board state? After is more tactical but slower.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in games where turn strength varies based on dice rolls, with players using resources or abilities to influence their action economy for crucial turns.
