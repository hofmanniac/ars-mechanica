## Overview

This combination creates a dual-resource system where players must balance action point budgets against card play decisions. Cards cost action points to play, creating constant tension between card quality and action quantity.

## How It Works

Players manage two interconnected resources each turn:

1. **Action points**: A fixed or variable budget of points to spend
2. **Hand of cards**: Cards that require action points to play

**Typical implementation**:
- Start turn with 3-5 action points and 5-7 cards in hand
- Each card shows an AP cost (often 1-3 points)
- Play cards by spending the required action points
- More powerful cards cost more AP; simpler cards cost less
- Unused AP and unplayed cards typically don't carry over

**Common variations**:
- **Fixed costs**: All cards cost 1 AP (emphasizes hand size)
- **Variable costs**: Cards range from 0-3+ AP (emphasizes cost efficiency)
- **Scaling effects**: Card power scales with AP invested
- **Free plays**: Some cards cost 0 AP but have conditions

## Why It Works

The dual-resource system creates compelling tactical decisions:

- **Quality vs. quantity**: One powerful 3-AP card or three 1-AP cards?
- **Efficiency puzzles**: Can you spend exactly your AP budget optimally?
- **Timing decisions**: Save AP for a future card, or spend now?
- **Opportunity cost**: Every card played means another cannot be

The combination prevents both card-draw luck dominance (you need AP to use cards) and pure math optimization (card variety adds tactical flexibility).

## Design Considerations

**Balance AP budgets**: Too few AP (2-3) limits meaningful choices; too many (8+) removes tension. Sweet spot is 4-6 AP with cards costing 1-2 AP mostly, occasional 3+ AP bombs.

**Hand size matters**: Larger hands (7+) reduce draw luck but increase analysis paralysis. Smaller hands (4-5) speed play but can feel limited.

**Leftover AP problem**: If players frequently end turns with unspent AP, either increase hand size, reduce costs, or add "spend any AP" catch-all actions.

**Card cost clarity**: Use clear iconography showing AP costs. Players need to quickly evaluate multiple play sequences without recounting.

**Combo potential**: Consider whether multi-card combos are desirable. If yes, ensure hand sizes support playing 2-3 cards per turn.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in games where cards represent actions and players have limited activation capacity per turn, requiring decisions about which cards to prioritize playing with available action points.
