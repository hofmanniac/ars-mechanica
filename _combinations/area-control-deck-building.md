## Overview

This combination lets players customize their territorial strategy through deck building, where cards enable area control actions. Deck composition determines which territorial plays are available each turn.

## How It Works

Cards drive territorial actions through various mechanisms:

**Card types for area control**:
- **Unit cards**: Deploy specific units to territories
- **Movement cards**: Move X units or move to specific regions
- **Claim cards**: Take control of territories meeting conditions
- **Combat cards**: Attack or defend in specific situations
- **Ability cards**: Special powers affecting territorial control

**Typical gameplay loop**:
1. Draw 5-7 cards from your personal deck
2. Play cards to take territorial actions
3. Acquire new cards during or between rounds
4. Shuffle acquired cards into your deck
5. Next turn, draw from your improved deck

**Deck specialization paths**:
- **Aggressive expansion**: Cards for claiming territories quickly
- **Defensive holding**: Cards for reinforcing and protecting areas
- **Mobile warfare**: Movement and repositioning cards
- **Combo strategies**: Cards that synergize for powerful plays

## Why It Works

The combination creates strategic depth across timeframes:

- **Short-term tactical**: Use cards in hand for territorial plays
- **Medium-term planning**: Acquire cards supporting your strategy
- **Long-term optimization**: Build a deck that enables your win condition

Deck building prevents static strategies — everyone starts similarly but customizes over time. Territorial competition creates urgency — you can't just optimize your deck in isolation.

## Design Considerations

**Starting decks**: Give all players identical weak decks (or draft starting decks) to create even starts. Include enough variety that players can take actions early.

**Card acquisition timing**: When can players buy cards? Mid-turn encourages responsive buying; end-of-round allows strategic planning. Consider what's visible during acquisition.

**Deck cycling speed**: Faster cycling (smaller decks, more drawing) makes acquisition matter quickly. Slower cycling adds long-term planning but delays payoff.

**Territory-card alignment**: Design cards that support different territorial strategies. Avoid one "best" deck archetype — support multiple viable paths (expansion, control, mobility).

**Hand size balance**: Larger hands (6-8) provide options but can cause analysis paralysis. Smaller hands (4-5) speed play but feel limiting with area control decisions.

**Acquisition currency**: What buys cards? Victory points creates catch-up difficulty; resources from territories creates rich-get-richer; separate economy balances both concerns.
