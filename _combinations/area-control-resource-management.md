## Overview

This combination ties resource generation directly to territorial control. The map becomes an economic asset — different regions produce different resources, and controlling them determines your resource economy. Players must balance military/territorial ambitions with economic needs, creating strategic tension between expansion, defense, and resource optimization.

## How It Works

**Territory-Based Production**:
1. Map divided into territories/regions
2. Each territory produces specific resources
3. Controlling territory grants its resource production
4. Resources used for expansion, development, or victory

**Production Systems**:

**Automatic Production**
- "At start of each round, collect resources from your territories"
- Passive income based on holdings
- Incentivizes expansion and defense

**Activated Production**
- "Take action to harvest resources from controlled territory"
- Active choice which territories to exploit
- Adds decision layer to resource generation

**Yield Variation**
- Different territories produce different amounts
- Rich territories (3 wood) vs. poor territories (1 wood)
- Creates "hot spots" worth fighting over

**Resource Diversity**
- Each territory produces specific resource type
- Must control multiple territories for diverse economy
- Drives expansion into different regions

**Random Production**
- Dice/cards determine which territories produce
- Catan-style number-based activation
- Adds uncertainty to economic planning

## Why It Works

This combination creates engaging strategic dynamics:

**Spatial Economic Planning**
- Map reveals resource distribution
- Plan expansion based on resource needs
- Geography determines strategy

**Contested Value**
- Valuable territories worth fighting for
- Creates conflict hotspots naturally
- Clear objectives (control the iron mountain!)

**Asymmetric Starts**
- Starting positions provide different resources
- Each player begins with unique economic situation
- Diverse strategies emerge from positions

**Visible Competition**
- Can see what resources opponents have access to
- Identify economic weaknesses to exploit
- Territorial control = economic transparency

**Expansion Incentive**
- Need more territories for more resources
- Natural drive toward conflict and competition
- Growth imperative built into mechanics

## Design Considerations

### Territory Resource Assignment

**How are resources distributed on map?**

**Clustered Distribution**
- Resources grouped by region (forest corner, mountain corner)
- Creates specialized territories
- Encourages focused control strategies

**Scattered Distribution**
- Resources distributed evenly across map
- Requires wide territorial presence
- Encourages expansion and sprawl

**Unique Territories**
- Each location produces different combination
- No two territories identical
- Every territory potentially valuable

**Redundant Production**
- Multiple territories produce same resource
- Reduces bottleneck frustration
- Makes territory loss less catastrophic

### Production Balance

**Resource scarcity levels**:

**Abundant Resources**
- Many territories produce (wood, food)
- Easy to access, less competition
- Base resources for economy

**Rare Resources**
- Few territories produce (gems, spices)
- Highly contested
- Strategic control points

**Balance**: Mix abundant basics with rare strategics creates layered competition.

### Territory Value

**All territories equal or varied?**

**Equal Value**
- All territories produce same amount
- Location/adjacency determines value
- Simple, symmetric

**Varied Value**
- Some territories rich, others poor
- Creates obvious priorities
- More interesting but can be unbalanced

**Variable Value**
- Value changes based on game state
- What you control affects what's valuable
- Dynamic, complex

### Control Mechanics

**How do you control territories?**

**Occupancy**
- Place units in territory to control
- Simple, visual
- Vulnerable to displacement

**Majority**
- Most units in territory = control
- Partial presence matters
- Encourages investment

**Building**
- Construct buildings/settlements to claim
- Permanent (or semi-permanent) control
- Reduces volatility

**Token Placement**
- Place control markers to claim
- Abstract, clean
- Can be more game-y

### Production Timing

**When do territories produce?**

**Start of Round**
- Collect from all territories automatically
- Simple, passive income
- Encourages hoarding

**Player Turn**
- Activate territories on your turn
- Active choice
- Can limit overproduction

**End of Round**
- Production after all actions taken
- Can't use resources immediately
- Planning layer

**Event-Driven**
- Specific triggers cause production
- Dynamic, unpredictable
- Can be exciting or frustrating

### Territory Contestation

**How hard is it to take territories?**

**Easy Capture**
- Move in, take control
- Volatile, dynamic
- High tension, potential kingmaking

**Moderate Capture**
- Combat/conflict required
- Balanced investment
- Strategic commitment

**Difficult Capture**
- Major investment to take territory
- Stable control
- Strategic, less tactical

**Permanent Capture**
- Once taken, cannot be retaken
- Extreme stability
- Early game decisive

**Balance**: Moderate difficulty maintains tension without excessive volatility.

### Economic Impact Scale

**How important are resources to winning?**

**Critical**
- Resources essential for victory
- Economic development = winning
- Territory control paramount

**Important**
- Resources help but aren't only path
- Balanced with other strategies
- Territory control valuable but not mandatory

**Supplementary**
- Resources provide advantages
- Other victory paths exist
- Territory control optional strategy

### Military-Economic Balance

**How much do resources support military vs. development?**

**Military Focus**
- Resources build armies to take more territories
- Expansionist gameplay
- Conflict-heavy

**Development Focus**
- Resources build economic improvements
- Engine-building gameplay
- Lower conflict

**Balanced**
- Resources support both military and development
- Strategic choice between expansion and improvement
- Multiple paths viable

## Strategic Layers

**Territorial Priorities**
- Which territories to control first?
- High-value targets vs. safe expansion
- Opportunistic captures vs. planned conquest

**Economic Diversification**
- Collect all resource types (flexible)
- Specialize in few resources (efficient)
- Adapt based on controlled territories

**Defensive Positioning**
- Protect valuable territories
- Consolidate holdings
- Create defensible borders

**Expansion Timing**
- When to push for new territories?
- When to consolidate holdings?
- Balance growth with security

**Resource Conversion**
- Use abundant resources to compensate for scarce
- Trade or convert between types
- Optimize production mix

## Common Pitfalls

**Runaway Territory Control**: Leader captures more territories, gets more resources, captures even more territories. Include defensive advantages or catch-up mechanisms.

**Starting Position Imbalance**: Some positions have access to better resources. Carefully balance map or use draft/auction for starting positions.

**Territory Stalemate**: If territories are too hard to capture, game becomes static. Ensure reasonable paths to territorial change.

**Obvious Best Territories**: If some territories clearly superior, everyone fights over them while others ignored. Balance resource values.

**Resource Overproduction**: Late game everyone has too many resources, they become meaningless. Include escalating costs or resource sinks.

**Military Dominance**: If military too strong, becomes pure war game. Balance military with economic/defensive advantages.

**Economic Solitaire**: If territories rarely change hands, becomes multiplayer optimization puzzle. Maintain territorial tension.

## Examples in Games

**Catan**: Control settlements/cities on hexes that produce resources (wood, brick, wheat, sheep, ore) when their number is rolled. Random production creates dynamic resource economy.

**Risk**: Control territories to collect armies based on continental holdings. Simple area majority for production (more territories = more armies).

**Scythe**: Control territories on board, each producing different resources. Use resources to build mechs, structures, and enlist recruits for territorial dominance.

**Small World**: Control regions that generate gold income. Gold used to field more troops, create buildings, and eventually score victory points. Regions have varied gold values.

**Blood Rage**: Control provinces during age to gain Rage (action currency). Provinces worth different amounts, and control shifts dramatically through warfare and pillaging.

**Dominant Species**: Place species in terrain hexes. During production phase, hexes produce resources for species present. Control competition determines resource allocation.
