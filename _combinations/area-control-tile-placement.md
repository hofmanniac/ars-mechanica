## Overview

This combination has players placing tiles to expand territorial holdings and compete for control of regions, creating a spatial puzzle with competitive stakes.

## How It Works

Tile placement drives territorial competition:

**Tile claiming mechanisms**:
- **Immediate claim**: Place tile, claim it with your marker
- **Build to claim**: Complete regions/patterns to claim them
- **Majority control**: Most tiles in area = control
- **Border control**: Tiles touching your territory expand it

**Scoring systems**:
- **Size scoring**: Larger territories score more
- **Feature completion**: Complete cities/roads/regions
- **Majority scoring**: Control most of a region type
- **Connection scoring**: Connected territory scores bonuses
- **Pattern scoring**: Specific tile arrangements score

**Territorial actions**:
- **Expand**: Place tiles adjacent to your holdings
- **Block**: Cut off opponent expansions
- **Steal**: Place in opponent territory to contest it
- **Encircle**: Surround opponent tiles
- **Bridge**: Connect separated territories

## Why It Works

The combination creates engaging competition:

- **Spatial puzzles**: Tiles must fit while advancing strategy
- **Interactive territory**: Can't ignore opponents' expansion
- **Multiple fronts**: Compete in different regions simultaneously
- **Opportunistic play**: Exploit opponent mistakes
- **Clear goals**: Territorial control is intuitive to understand

Spatial tile placement adds puzzle-solving to territorial competition, preventing pure conflict from dominating.

## Design Considerations

**Tile draw randomness**: Random draws create uncertainty (exciting but can frustrate); hand selection gives control (strategic but less dramatic). Consider hybrid: draw 2, place 1.

**Claim timing**: Claiming when placing tiles is simple; claiming after completing regions rewards planning. Balance immediacy vs. strategy.

**Territory permanence**: Can claimed territory be stolen/contested? Permanent claims reward early aggression; contestable claims maintain tension.

**Tile placement rules**: Open placement (place anywhere) is simple but chaotic. Adjacent-only creates organic growth. Edge-matching adds puzzle constraints.

**Scoring frequency**: Score during game (ongoing feedback) vs. end game (long-term planning). During-game prevents runaway leaders; end-game rewards big strategies.

**Board size**: Fixed board limits expansion and forces competition. Growing board allows everyone to expand but reduces interaction.

**Territory visibility**: Clear ownership markers essential. Color-code tiles or use meeples/markers to show control.

**Player count scaling**: More players = more competition for space. Ensure enough tiles/board space that 4-5 players don't gridlock immediately.

## Examples in Games

This combination is the foundation of many tile-laying games where players build territories by placing tiles and score based on the size, type, or majority control of regions they create.
