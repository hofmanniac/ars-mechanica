## Overview

This combination applies deck-building principles to dice, where players acquire custom dice or upgrade die faces over time, building an optimized dice pool that generates better results than starting dice.

## How It Works

Instead of building card decks, players build dice pools:

**Dice acquisition methods**:
- **Buy whole dice**: Purchase new dice with unique face combinations
- **Upgrade faces**: Replace faces on existing dice with better symbols
- **Add to pool**: Start with 4-6 dice, acquire more powerful ones
- **Remove weak dice**: Trim starting dice as you acquire better ones

**Typical gameplay**:
1. Roll your current dice pool (5-8 dice)
2. Use manipulation (rerolls, changing faces) to improve results
3. Resolve actions based on die faces showing
4. Acquire new dice or upgrades using resources/VP
5. Next turn, roll your improved pool

**Die face types**:
- **Resources**: Generate money, materials, etc.
- **Actions**: Enable specific moves or plays
- **Multipliers**: Boost other results
- **Wilds**: Flexible symbols
- **Blanks/Duds**: Weaknesses to overcome

## Why It Works

The combination creates satisfying progression:

- **Visual progression**: Physically adding custom dice shows growth
- **Probability improvement**: Better dice pools = consistently better rolls
- **Specialization**: Focus on dice supporting your strategy
- **Tension remains**: Even optimized pools have variance
- **Manipulation matters**: Good manipulation use maximizes any pool

Dice crafting maintains deck-building's optimization puzzle while adding dice rolling's tactile excitement and push-your-luck elements.

## Design Considerations

**Starting dice balance**: Give everyone identical weak starting dice (mix of okay faces with some blanks). This creates even starts and motivation to upgrade.

**Die face density**: How many symbols per die? 6-sided dice with 2-3 symbol types are readable. More complex faces (d8, d10) risk analysis paralysis.

**Pool size scaling**: Does the pool grow (add dice) or transform (replace dice)? Growing pools create increasingly powerful rolls; transforming pools maintain tension.

**Acquisition costs**: Price powerful dice appropriately. Too cheap means everyone maxes out; too expensive removes progression satisfaction. Consider VP costs vs. resource costs.

**Manipulation economy**: Balance manipulation access with dice quality. If manipulation is unlimited, dice quality matters less. If too limited, luck dominates.

**Combo potential**: Should dice face combinations create bonuses? ("If you roll 3+ of same symbol...") This adds depth but complexity.

**Physical concerns**: Custom dice with stickers/sleeves are cheaper but less premium feeling. Engraved dice cost more. Consider production budget.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in dice-based games where players gradually improve their dice pools through acquisition and upgrades, creating better probability distributions over time.
