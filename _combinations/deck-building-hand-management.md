## Overview

This combination layers tactical hand management on top of strategic deck building, requiring players to optimize both acquisition decisions and play decisions for maximum efficiency.

## How It Works

Players face decisions at two levels:

**Deck building decisions** (strategic):
- Which cards to acquire for your deck
- Balancing card types and costs
- Building synergies and combos
- Deck thinning vs. deck diversification

**Hand management decisions** (tactical):
- Which cards to play this turn vs. hold
- What order to play cards (sequencing)
- When to use discard/draw abilities
- Managing hand size limits

**Key hand management elements**:
- **Card combos**: Cards work better together ("If you play A, B costs less")
- **Sequencing**: Play order matters ("Next card you play gains +2")
- **Holding benefits**: Some cards stronger held until right moment
- **Discard costs**: Discarding cards may enable abilities
- **Hand size**: Limited hand size forces difficult discard choices

## Why It Works

The dual optimization creates skill expression:

- **Strategic mastery**: Building decks that enable strong hands
- **Tactical mastery**: Playing hands optimally with sequencing and timing
- **Synergy discovery**: Finding card combinations during play
- **Adaptation**: Sometimes optimal deck-building strategy fails due to draws

Good deck builders can't sleepwalk to victory — they must play hands well. Good tacticians can't overcome terrible decks — they must build strategically.

## Design Considerations

**Hand size sweet spot**: 5-7 cards provides meaningful choices without overwhelming players. Larger hands (8+) amplify deck building but risk analysis paralysis.

**Draw timing**: When do players draw? Draw-5-at-start-of-turn is simple but limits tactical drawing. Drawing during turn (after each play) adds complexity but more interesting decisions.

**Discard mechanics**: If players discard down to hand size at end of turn, they won't hold cards. Consider "keep hand" or "discard only if over limit" rules.

**Combo obviousness**: Should card combos be explicit ("If you have Card A, this costs less") or emergent (players discover synergies)? Explicit is clearer; emergent rewards mastery.

**Shuffling triggers**: When does deck shuffle? Immediate shuffle after playing cards vs. end-of-turn shuffle affects hand planning significantly.

**Acquisition timing**: Acquiring mid-turn allows reactive buying; end-of-round allows strategic planning based on what you need.

**Deck size management**: Can players thin decks? Smaller decks cycle faster (more consistent) but less variety. Larger decks are less consistent but more strategic options.

## Examples in Games

This combination is the foundation of many deck-building games where cards form hands and players must decide both what to acquire and how to play their hands optimally each turn.
