## Overview

This combination applies deck-building optimization principles to tile placement, where players acquire tiles for their personal pool and draw from that pool each turn, improving their placement options over time.

## How It Works

Tile pools work like card decks but with spatial components:

**Pool building**:
- Start with basic tiles in your personal bag/deck
- Acquire new tiles during or between rounds
- Add acquired tiles to your pool
- Draw from pool each turn for placement options

**Typical turn structure**:
1. Draw 2-4 tiles from your personal pool
2. Place tiles following spatial rules
3. Score based on tile placement
4. Acquire new tiles using resources/points
5. Next turn, draw from improved pool

**Tile specialization paths**:
- **Shape focus**: Acquire tiles that fit your board configuration
- **Scoring focus**: Tiles that score well with your strategy
- **Flexibility**: Versatile tiles that fit many situations
- **Combo tiles**: Tiles that synergize with others you own

**Pool management**:
- **Bag-building**: Draw from opaque bag (truly random)
- **Deck-building**: Draw from shuffled deck (predictable cycles)
- **Tile removal**: Can you remove weak starting tiles?

## Why It Works

The combination creates satisfying strategic progression:

- **Customization**: Your tile pool reflects your strategy
- **Improved consistency**: Better pools = better placement options
- **Spatial puzzles**: Still must place tiles cleverly from what you draw
- **Long-term planning**: Acquire tiles that fit your developing board
- **Variety**: Each game develops differently based on acquisitions

Players can't just buy the "best" tiles — tiles must work with their specific board configuration and strategy.

## Design Considerations

**Starting tiles**: Give all players identical basic sets — simple shapes that fit anywhere but score poorly. This creates even starts and upgrade motivation.

**Draw count**: Drawing 2-3 tiles gives meaningful choice without overwhelming. 1 tile feels too limiting; 5+ tiles creates analysis paralysis in spatial puzzles.

**Tile variety**: Offer enough tile types (15-30) that players can specialize differently. Too few means everyone acquires the same tiles.

**Pool size management**: Should pools grow indefinitely or stay fixed size? Growing pools mean more draws but decreased consistency. Fixed size (replace old tiles) maintains tight control.

**Acquisition costs**: Price tiles by power and versatility. Common tiles (fit anywhere) should cost less than specialized high-scorers.

**Spatial considerations**: Tiles that only fit specific situations should cost less or score more to compensate for restrictiveness.

**Bag vs. deck**: Bag-building is simpler (always random) but requires physical components. Deck-building allows cycle counting but adds complexity.

**Physical production**: Custom tiles are expensive to produce. Consider cardboard tiles vs. wooden vs. plastic based on budget.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in tile-laying games where players build personal tile pools and draw from them, gradually customizing their available placement options.
