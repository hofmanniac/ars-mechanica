## Overview

This combination treats cards as workers, creating a unique fusion where deck-building choices directly determine worker placement options. Each card represents a worker with specific abilities or placement restrictions.

## How It Works

Players build a deck of worker cards throughout the game, similar to traditional deck-builders. Each turn:

1. Draw a hand of cards from your personal deck
2. Play cards from hand to place workers on action spaces
3. Worker cards may have unique abilities, placement restrictions, or bonus effects
4. Action spaces follow standard worker placement blocking rules
5. Acquire new worker cards to add to your deck, improving future turns

The deck acts as your labor pool — better cards mean more powerful or flexible workers, but you're limited by what you draw each turn.

## Why It Works

This combination creates compelling tension between **long-term optimization** and **short-term tactics**:

- **Deck quality vs. draw luck**: A well-tuned deck is powerful, but you're at the mercy of your draws
- **Specialization vs. flexibility**: Focused decks are efficient but vulnerable to blocking
- **Investment timing**: When do you stop improving your deck and start executing your strategy?

The competitive placement element prevents the combination from becoming a solitaire optimization puzzle. Even with perfect deck construction, you must react to opponent placements and adapt your plans.

## Design Considerations

**Balance acquisition and placement**: Ensure players have meaningful placement decisions even early in the game when decks are weak. Starting hands should enable basic strategies.

**Card clarity**: Worker cards need clear iconography showing which spaces they can access or what bonuses they provide. Players need to quickly evaluate their hand.

**Deck size management**: Consider whether players can thin their decks (remove weak cards) or if deck bloat is intentional tension. Larger decks reduce consistency but increase variety.

**Placement variety**: Provide enough action spaces that multiple viable strategies exist. Avoid single "must-have" spaces that cause excessive blocking frustration.

## Examples in Games

Popular implementations of this combination include deck-builders where cards represent workers or enable actions, and worker placement games where workers come from a customizable pool rather than a fixed set.
