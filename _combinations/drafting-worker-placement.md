## Overview

This combination has players draft workers before using them for placement, creating asymmetric player powers and strategic worker pools that differ from player to player.

## How It Works

Worker drafting shapes the entire game:

**Draft timing options**:
- **Pre-game draft**: Draft all workers before game starts
- **Round-by-round**: Draft new workers each round
- **Acquisition**: Buy/draft workers during gameplay

**Worker differentiation**:
- **Ability workers**: Special powers when placed
- **Access workers**: Can access specific action spaces
- **Strength workers**: Variable strength (1-3 power)
- **Bonus workers**: Provide resources or bonuses
- **Restriction workers**: Limited placement options but powerful

**Typical implementation**:
1. Draft phase: Players draft 3-5 worker cards
2. Placement phase: Use drafted workers on action spaces
3. Workers may have unique abilities or restrictions
4. Everyone has different worker capabilities

## Why It Works

Drafting creates asymmetric gameplay with skilled decision-making:

- **Strategic identity**: Your workers define your strategy
- **Adaptation**: Draft workers that fit the board/opponents
- **Specialization**: Focus on worker types that synergize
- **Denial**: Draft workers opponents need
- **Variety**: Every game feels different based on drafted workers

Players aren't locked into strategies — they choose during drafting — but must commit to their choices and make them work.

## Design Considerations

**Worker power balance**: All workers should be viable in some strategy. Avoid clearly "best" workers that get drafted first every time. Power = flexibility × strength.

**Draft pool size**: Offer 2-3x more workers than players will draft. Too few (1.5x) limits choice; too many (5x) creates analysis paralysis.

**Snake draft vs. pass draft**: Snake draft (1-2-2-1) is simpler and fairer. Pass draft (hands going around) supports more players and signals.

**Worker diversity**: 15-30 unique workers provides enough variety. Too few means similar games; too many overwhelms new players.

**Action space design**: With unique workers, action spaces should support multiple worker types. Avoid "this space only works with Worker X" situations.

**Worker count per player**: 3-5 workers is sweet spot. 2 workers feels limiting; 6+ workers dilutes specialization.

**Variable player powers**: Should workers provide asymmetric powers or just different optimization paths? Powers add theme but complexity.

**Display clarity**: Workers need clear iconography showing abilities and restrictions. Players reference these constantly.

## Examples in Games

This combination appears in worker placement games where workers have unique abilities or access, drafted at the start or acquired during gameplay, creating specialized labor forces per player.
