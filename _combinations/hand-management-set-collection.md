## Overview

This classic combination forms the foundation of rummy-style games, where players secretly collect cards in their hand and reveal completed sets for points. Simple rules hide deep tactical decisions about what to keep, what to discard, and when to reveal.

## How It Works

The core loop is elegant:

1. **Draw**: Take a card from the draw deck or discard pile
2. **Organize**: Arrange your hand toward completing sets (runs, matches, etc.)
3. **Discard**: Choose one card to discard, revealing information to opponents
4. **Reveal**: When ready, lay down completed sets to score points
5. **Go out**: First player to use all cards (or meet a condition) ends the round

**Set types commonly used**:
- **Runs**: Consecutive numbers in the same suit (7-8-9 of Hearts)
- **Groups**: Same rank across different suits (three Kings)
- **Color groups**: Same color or attribute
- **Custom patterns**: Themed sets specific to the game

## Why It Works

The combination creates accessible yet deep gameplay:

- **Hidden information**: Your hand is secret, creating tension about opponent progress
- **Observable signals**: Discards and draws reveal hints about opponent strategies
- **Risk vs. reward**: Hold cards for bigger sets, or play safe to avoid getting caught?
- **Interactive solitaire**: Build your own sets while actively reading and blocking opponents

Players can grasp the basics in seconds ("make matching sets") but master reads on opponent hands through careful observation over time.

## Design Considerations

**Hand size limits**: Larger hands offer more options but slow decision-making. 7-10 cards is typical for good flow.

**Set scoring**: Should partial sets score? Should revealed sets be protected or vulnerable? Balance rewarding fast play vs. building big hands.

**Discard pile rules**: Letting players take from discards adds interaction but can cause AP (analysis paralysis). Consider "only the last discard" or "only on your turn" restrictions.

**Going out penalties**: Players caught with cards in hand need meaningful penalties, or everyone waits for perfect hands. Unmelded cards usually count as negative points.

**Special cards**: Wilds, skips, and other special cards add spice but can overwhelm new players. Start simple.

## Examples in Games

Classic implementations include traditional Rummy, Gin Rummy, Rummikub (with tiles), Phase 10 (progressive sets), and modern variations that add themes or additional mechanics while keeping the core draw-discard-meld structure.
