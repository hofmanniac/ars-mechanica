## What This Dimension Represents

Timing & Structure is the temporal backbone of your game. It answers: **"How does play unfold over time?"**

This dimension determines rhythm, pacing, and the granularity of decision-making. Whether players take turns sequentially, act simultaneously, or race against the clock shapes the entire play experience. The structure you choose affects everything from strategic planning to social dynamics at the table.

Every game exists in time, making this one of the most foundational design dimensions. Your choices here create the framework within which all other mechanics operate.

## Common Mechanics

These are the most frequently used approaches to structuring gameplay:

### Turn Order
Players take actions one at a time in a predetermined sequence. Classic and intuitive, allows for careful planning and clear state observation. Can suffer from downtime between turns.

### Round Structure
Games divide into discrete rounds, each containing a sequence of phases or player turns. Provides natural breakpoints for cleanup, scoring, or state changes. Creates rhythm and anticipation.

### Simultaneous Action Selection
All players choose actions at once, then reveal and resolve together. Eliminates downtime but reduces information about opponent choices. Speeds up play significantly.

### Action Timer
Real-time games where players act as fast as they can within a time limit. Creates urgency and excitement but can favor quick thinkers over deep strategists.

### Variable Turn Order
Turn sequence changes based on game state, player choices, or bidding. Introduces strategic decisions about when to act. Common in worker placement and economic games.

## Uncommon Implementations

These creative approaches to timing demonstrate alternative design space:

### I Cut, You Choose
**Example: Favor of the Pharaoh**
One player divides resources or options into groups, another player chooses first. Alternates roles. Creates tension between division and selection.

### Interrupt Mechanics
**Example: Magic: The Gathering**
Players can react during opponents' turns with instant-speed actions. Maintains engagement during all players' turns but increases complexity.

### Sand Timer
**Example: Escape: The Curse of the Temple**
Physical timer creates absolute deadline pressure. Everyone acts simultaneously until time expires. Frantic and social.

### Programmed Actions
**Example: RoboRally, Colt Express**
Players pre-commit to sequences of future actions, then watch them unfold. Planning happens simultaneously, execution sequentially. Creates comedy and chaos.

### Elastic Rounds
**Example: Millennium Blades**
Round length varies based on player actions or triggers. Game adapts pacing to player behavior. Rewards efficiency or creates interesting time pressure.

### Interrupt Points
**Example: Race for the Galaxy**
Players act simultaneously until predetermined checkpoints where all must synchronize. Balances speed with coordination needs.

## Dimensional Intersections

Timing & Structure interacts fundamentally with nearly every dimension:

### × Action Selection
How actions are chosen is deeply tied to when they're chosen.
- Simultaneous Selection + Hidden Actions = Tension and prediction
- Sequential Turns + Full Information = Perfect information strategy

### × Player Interaction
Timing determines when and how players can affect each other.
- Simultaneous Play = Less direct interference, more collision
- Turn-Based + Interrupts = Constant engagement but complex

### × Uncertainty & Information
When information is revealed shapes strategic planning.
- Simultaneous Reveal = Hidden information until resolution
- Sequential Turns = Information updates continuously

### × Resource Economy
Timing of resource generation and spending creates rhythm.
- Income Phase Each Round = Predictable economic growth
- Variable Timing = Opportunity costs and speculation

## Design Questions

When working with this dimension, consider:

- **What's the right granularity?** Do players take one action per turn or multiple? How long is a round?
- **How do you minimize downtime?** Can players plan during others' turns? Are there simultaneous elements?
- **What creates pacing?** Does the game accelerate, maintain steady rhythm, or vary intentionally?
- **How do players synchronize?** Are there natural breakpoints for regrouping and socializing?
- **What's your target play time?** How does your temporal structure support or undermine this goal?

## Patterns & Pitfalls

### Successful Patterns

**Phase-Based Rounds**
Clear structure where each round follows the same sequence of phases. Easy to learn, creates predictable rhythm. Example: Agricola's work phase, then harvest.

**Flexible Turn Order**
Players gain agency over when they act. Adds strategic layer without excessive complexity. Example: Variable player order based on position.

**Simultaneous with Reveals**
Everyone plans secretly, then reveals together. Eliminates downtime while maintaining surprise. Example: Diplomacy orders.

**Increasing Tempo**
Game accelerates toward climax. Creates rising tension and prevents late-game drag. Example: Shorter rounds or more frequent events.

### Common Pitfalls

**Analysis Paralysis**
Long turns with many options cause extended downtime. Consider timers, simpler decisions, or simultaneous play.

**Meaningless Phases**
Empty phases that only sometimes matter waste time. Consolidate or make conditional.

**Unclear Structure**
Players don't know what comes next or when the round ends. Need clear signposting and consistent structure.

**Pacing Mismatch**
Game structure doesn't match strategic depth. Quick decisions in slow turns, or rushed strategy in fast play.

**Runaway Length**
Game duration becomes unpredictable or extends too long. Need clear ending conditions and pacing controls.

## Examples in Practice

**7 Wonders** uses simultaneous card selection to keep all players engaged constantly. Rounds have clear structure with age transitions providing natural breaks. Play time remains consistent regardless of player count.

**Root** has asymmetric turn structure where different factions follow different timing rules. Some act in traditional turns, others have special timing windows. Creates unique gameplay experiences.

**Wingspan** uses round structure with decreasing action allowances - players get fewer actions in later rounds. This naturally accelerates the game and prevents lengthy endgames.

**Space Base** keeps all players engaged during every turn through passive resource generation, eliminating the downtime problem of traditional turn-based games.

**Captain Sonar** pushes real-time play to the extreme with simultaneous team-based action for both teams at once. Creates intense, energetic gameplay that feels unlike traditional board games.
