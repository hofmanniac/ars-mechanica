## What It Is

Resource Management encompasses the systems through which players:
- **Acquire** resources through various means
- **Store** resources within capacity limits
- **Convert** resources from one type to another
- **Spend** resources to take actions or score points
- **Balance** competing demands for limited resources

Resources can be tangible (wood, gold, food) or abstract (influence, time, energy). The mechanic creates strategic tension through scarcity — players never have enough resources to do everything they want.

## How It Works

### Resource Acquisition

**Production**: Resources generate automatically each round
- Territory control (Catan — roll dice, territories produce)
- Buildings/cards (Terraforming Mars — production tracks increase)
- Turn income (many games — "collect 2 gold at start of turn")

**Harvesting**: Take actions to collect resources
- Worker placement (claim resource spaces)
- Area control (control regions to extract resources)
- Card/tile effects (play cards that generate resources)

**Rewards**: Gain resources as compensation for actions
- Completing objectives or quests
- Selling goods or services
- Trading with other players or the game

**Conversion**: Transform resources into other resources
- 2 wood → 1 brick
- 3 ore + 2 coal → 1 iron
- Often through buildings, actions, or special abilities

### Resource Storage

**Unlimited Storage**: No cap on resources (simpler, removes pressure)

**Limited Storage**: Hard caps requiring management
- General limit (max 10 resources total)
- Per-type limits (max 5 wood, max 3 stone)
- Expandable storage (build warehouses to increase capacity)

**Resource Degradation**: Resources decay or spoil
- Food spoils each round
- Temporary resources disappear if not used
- Creates urgency and pressure

### Resource Spending

**Building Costs**: Resources required to construct improvements
- Fixed costs (always costs 2 wood + 1 brick)
- Variable costs (costs scale with what you've built)

**Action Costs**: Pay resources to take actions
- Movement costs
- Special ability activation
- Bidding/auction participation

**Maintenance**: Ongoing resource expenses
- Feed workers/armies each round
- Upkeep costs for buildings
- Taxation or overhead

**Victory Points**: Direct resource-to-points conversion
- End-game scoring (1 point per gold)
- During-game purchases (buy victory point cards)

## Strategic Depth

### Core Decisions

**Acquisition vs. Spending**: When to collect resources vs. when to use them
- Building resource engines early (investment)
- Taking immediate benefits (opportunity)
- Balancing both approaches

**Resource Type Priority**: Which resources to pursue
- Identifying bottleneck resources (what you lack most)
- Focusing on synergistic resources
- Opportunistic collection (take what's available)

**Conversion Efficiency**: Optimizing resource transformations
- Finding cost-effective conversion paths
- Building conversion engines
- Timing conversions to minimize waste

**Capacity Management**: Handling storage limitations
- Preventing resource loss from caps
- Expanding storage strategically
- Using resources before they're wasted

**Diversification vs. Specialization**
- Collecting all resource types (flexible, resilient)
- Focusing on specific resources (efficient, vulnerable)
- Trading to convert between types

## Design Considerations

### Resource Types

**How many resource types?**
- **Few (1-3)**: Simple to track, limited strategic depth
- **Moderate (4-6)**: Good variety without overwhelming
- **Many (7+)**: Rich optimization space but complex tracking

**Resource relationships**:
- **Independent**: Each resource has distinct uses
- **Hierarchical**: Basic → intermediate → advanced resources
- **Convertible**: Resources can be exchanged or transformed
- **Substitutable**: Some resources can replace others

### Acquisition Balance

**Predictable vs. Random**:
- **Predictable**: Players can plan multi-turn strategies
- **Random**: Adaptation and flexibility rewarded
- **Hybrid**: Base production + random bonuses

**Abundance vs. Scarcity**:
- **Abundant**: Resources flow freely, spending is the puzzle
- **Scarce**: Every resource matters, careful planning required
- **Variable**: Changes throughout game (early scarcity, late abundance)

**Active vs. Passive**:
- **Active**: Players take actions to collect resources
- **Passive**: Resources generate automatically
- **Mixed**: Some passive income, some active collection

### Resource Sink Quality

**Are spending decisions interesting?**
- Multiple valuable uses for same resources
- Timing matters (early vs. late game value)
- Opportunity cost is significant
- Avoid obvious optimal paths

**Conversion rates**:
- Balanced exchange rates (no strictly superior resource)
- Asymmetric value (resources worth different amounts)
- Context-dependent value (resources valuable in different situations)

### Storage Mechanics

**Should resources be limited?**
- **Unlimited**: Removes tracking burden, allows hoarding
- **Limited**: Creates pressure to spend, adds decisions
- **Expandable**: Capacity building becomes strategy element

**Waste vs. Preservation**:
- Lost resources feel bad (frustrating)
- Storage pressure creates urgency (engaging)
- Balance player agency with meaningful constraints

### Game Pacing

**Early game**: Typically resource-starved
- Slow start builds tension
- Early choices have weight
- Can frustrate if too restrictive

**Mid game**: Resource flow increases
- Players execute strategies
- Engines come online
- Sweet spot of scarcity and abundance

**Late game**: Often resource-rich
- Players can execute big moves
- Focus shifts from gathering to optimal spending
- Risk: becomes trivial if too abundant

## Common Variations

### Resource Conversion Chains

Resources transform through multiple steps:
- Raw materials → processed goods → finished products
- Each tier more valuable but requires investment
- Example: Wood → Lumber → Furniture → Victory Points

### Resource Wildcards

Some resources act as "any type":
- Gold can substitute for any resource
- Joker tokens provide flexibility
- Typically expensive or rare

### Shared vs. Personal Resources

**Personal resources**: Only you can use them
- Clear ownership, no interaction
- Can feel like multiplayer solitaire

**Shared resources**: Common pool or marketplace
- Competition for limited supply
- Player interaction through scarcity

**Hybrid**: Some personal, some shared
- Personal resources for basic needs
- Shared resources for competitive elements

### Resource Denial

Can players prevent opponents from getting resources?
- **No denial**: Everyone can get what they need (low conflict)
- **Indirect denial**: Taking resources before others (competition)
- **Direct denial**: Actively blocking or stealing (confrontational)

## Strategic Layers

**Economic Planning**: Multi-turn resource planning
- What resources will I need in 3 turns?
- Building toward specific goals
- Adapting plans based on availability

**Efficiency Optimization**: Getting maximum value
- Finding optimal conversion paths
- Minimizing waste
- Identifying synergies

**Tempo Control**: Pacing resource acquisition and spending
- Racing opponents to key resources
- Timing big moves
- Balancing development speed

**Risk Management**: Handling uncertainty
- Hedging against bad luck
- Diversifying resource streams
- Having backup plans

**Opponent Awareness**: Adapting to others' strategies
- Competing for scarce resources
- Denying critical resources to opponents
- Identifying what opponents need

## Common Pitfalls

**Too Many Resources**: 8+ types become hard to track and manage. Keep it to 6 or fewer unless tracking is automated.

**Obvious Bottlenecks**: If one resource is always scarce while others abundant, it becomes the only meaningful resource. Balance acquisition rates.

**Accounting Tedium**: Constantly adding/subtracting small amounts slows gameplay. Use production tracks or batch updates.

**Runaway Economies**: Rich players get richer. Include catch-up mechanisms or caps on production.

**Boring Optimization**: If optimal resource strategy is clear, no meaningful decisions exist. Ensure multiple viable paths.

**Resource Hoarding**: If hoarding is optimal strategy, game becomes about who collects most rather than who uses best. Add storage limits or spoilage.

**Fiddly Components**: Too many small tokens to track. Use quality components or streamlined tracking methods.

## Why Use This Mechanic

Resource Management excels at:
- **Creating strategic depth** through optimization puzzles
- **Forcing meaningful choices** via scarcity
- **Providing clear goals** (I need 3 more wood...)
- **Enabling planning** (predictable resource flows)
- **Supporting varied strategies** (different resource paths)
- **Generating player interaction** (competition for resources)
- **Teaching economic thinking** (supply, demand, opportunity cost)

The mechanic is intuitive (everyone understands collecting and spending) while supporting deep strategic gameplay. It's a foundation for countless other mechanics and game systems.

## Combining with Other Mechanics

Resource Management pairs naturally with:
- **Worker Placement**: Workers collect resources from action spaces
- **Engine Building**: Build engines that generate resources
- **Trading**: Exchange resources with other players
- **Area Control**: Control territories that produce resources
- **Set Collection**: Collect resource combinations for bonuses
- **Action Points**: Spend resources or actions (dual economy)
